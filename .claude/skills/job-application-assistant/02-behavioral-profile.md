## Overview
Sahil is a **builder-operator** pattern: an applied AI engineer who measures himself by systems that reach production and stay up, not by demos. His edge is a non-linear path (Mechanical engineering → self-taught → C-DAC → 5 years of production GenAI) that he frames as an asset - he learns hard things fast and explains them simply.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Ownership / end-to-end delivery | High | Takes projects from POC to production and hands off working, operated systems |
| Autonomy | High | Works best with room to build; prefers greenfield over long-cycle maintenance |
| Communication / teaching | High | A year teaching AI & Python internationally; explains complex systems simply |
| Frontier drive | High | Gravitates to the cutting edge of applied AI (agentic tooling, retrieval, LLMOps) |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Ships to production, not notebooks:** Scaled an incident-assist platform 20 → 200+ SREs; leads with measured numbers and honestly flags estimates.
- **Diagnoses and rebuilds hard problems:** Found why cosine search failed on near-duplicate data and rebuilt it as hybrid retrieval, roughly doubling recall.
- **Builds in public / communicates clearly:** Runs a visibility pipeline (LinkedIn/GitHub/X) and a portfolio site that itself ships live AI features.

## How You Work Best
- Frontier-AI teams with real production surface and fast shipping cycles
- High autonomy and end-to-end ownership of a problem
- Remote-friendly, async, communication-heavy environments

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Prefers greenfield build over long maintenance:** Frame as bias toward high-leverage, net-new work - pair with willingness to operate what he ships (he did the incident-platform handoff and training).
- **Intellectually honest about metrics to a fault:** Distinguishes measured numbers from estimates. Frame as rigor and credibility, not hedging.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "ownership", "end-to-end", "0-to-1 / POC to production", "ship fast"
- "applied AI", "GenAI", "LLM/RAG in production", "agentic"
- "autonomy", "remote", "async", "build in public"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "maintenance-heavy", "legacy support", "keeping the lights on"
- "pure research", "notebook prototyping only", "no production ownership"
- rigid, process-heavy environments with little autonomy

## Management Style Preferences
- Works well with managers who set direction and get out of the way
- Values technical peers who care about production quality and honest metrics
- Less suited to micromanagement or approval-gated, slow-shipping cultures

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Lead with the POC → production narrative and one flagship (incident assistant or Convogene). Weave in the teaching/communication angle for remote roles.
- **CV:** Emphasize measured outcomes (triage -70%, 20 → 200+ SREs, recall ~30% → ~80%) and production infra (Kafka/K8s/Airflow).
- **Interviews:** Use the STAR examples in `07-interview-prep.md`; lead with the confidence-tagged numbers from the evidence bank.
- **Don't overstate:** Never present the MTTR -40-60% estimate or "near-zero hallucination" as measured facts - both are estimates/qualitative. Say "beta", not "full production", for the 200+-SRE figure.
